This project was honestly more work than I expected, but in a good way. Pulling everything together into one cohesive site made me realize how much I actually learned over the semester. The hardest part was making all the pages feel consistent — getting the navigation, colors, and layout to match across everything took more time than building the pages themselves. But once it came together, it felt really satisfying to see it as one complete site instead of a bunch of separate files.

Testing on different browsers and devices was eye-opening. Things that looked fine on Chrome would be slightly off on Firefox, and the mobile layout needed more adjustments than I thought. It made me appreciate why responsive design matters — you can't just build for one screen and call it done. The media query stuff I learned in class actually made a lot more sense once I was applying it to my own site.

Looking back at the whole course, I went from barely understanding HTML structure to building multi-page sites with external stylesheets, responsive layouts, and proper accessibility. CSS was definitely the most challenging part for me, but it's also what makes the biggest difference visually. I feel a lot more confident now about building websites from scratch, and I can actually see how these skills connect to everything else I'm studying in IT.
